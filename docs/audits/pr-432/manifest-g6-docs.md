**G6-4: IMPORTANT** (`docs/adr/0035-operational-aggregate-monitoring.md:311-313,318-323`; `docs/rls-exemption-register.md`, the `jobs.payload via job_payload` row; `docs/REKODA_LAUNCH_READINESS.md:142` (xxii); `docs/HANDOFF.md:20`)

- **The claims:** these say an "unpinned, cross-tenant or bulk payload read on the worker credential is refused by the database", that the worker "can no longer read payloads ... in bulk", and that the amendment "grants nothing new".
- **What 0228 actually does:** `job_payload(p_job)` (0228 l.30-40) is a per-call check against the current `app.business_id`. The worker holds SELECT on `jobs.id` and `business_id` for every tenant (0228 l.45-48; `worker_claim`).
- **Ways around it:**
  - (a) Under one pin, `SELECT job_payload(id) FROM jobs WHERE business_id = <pinned>` returns every payload of that business in one statement. That is known R07; `jobs.ts:684` uses exactly this shape.
  - (b) New: `set_config` is executable by PUBLIC, so `SELECT set_config('app.business_id', business_id::text, true), job_payload(id) FROM jobs` re-pins per row and plausibly returns every tenant's payloads in one statement. Not executed here (no database access).
  - (c) Settlement's EXECUTE reads any kind's payload, bypassing `settlement_only_its_kinds` (0217 l.180). That is known R01, and the working tree's uncommitted 0231 targets it.
  - (d) 0228 and 0229 grant new EXECUTE privileges, so "grants nothing new" is false.
- **Tests:** `job-payload-isolation.integration.test.ts` has no bulk, re-pin or settlement-kind case.
- **Failure scenario:** the owner accepts Amendment 6 at P12 on the strength of a database guarantee that does not hold. The residual is wider than stated, and OWN-94 (2) requires it to be documented accurately.
- **Fix:** restate the residual: per-call pin check only; a holder of the worker credential can read every payload, one business per statement or by re-pinning within one statement; settlement can read any kind until a kind filter is added. Remove "grants nothing new". Add RED tests for (a) to (c) or record them as accepted.
- **Confidence:** high for (a), (c) and (d); medium-high for (b).

**G6-6: IMPORTANT** (`docs/adr/0024-the-commercial-terms.md` §Refunds; `apps/web/src/app/refunds/page.tsx:52-58`; `packages/db/migrations/0230_refund_through_the_operator.sql:399-426`; OWN-94 (8))

- **What is wrong:** the public refunds page and ADR 0024 promise "Full refund of the incorrect charge" and "Full or part refund" when Rekoda failed the merchant. `subscription_record_refund` refuses any record for a credited pack that is used, partly refunded, older than 7 days or credited before 0230. Its message, "settle it by hand", names no sanctioned path: since 0230 no runtime role can write the refund columns, so only the database owner can. OWN-94 (8) narrows ADR 0024 for packs, but no superseding note reconciles the two rows.
- **Failure scenario:** a merchant's credited and partly used pack is lost to a Rekoda outage. The operator refunds it in Paystack as the published policy promises, and Rekoda's record stays `paid` with its units credited. That is a silent discrepancy between the provider's truth and Rekoda's books (invariant 6), or else an owner-level SQL edit.
- **Fix:** an owner decision is needed. Record in OWNER_DECISIONS and LAUNCH_READINESS how a credited pack is refunded under the service-failure and incorrect-charge rows (an operator path, or a published exception), and align `/refunds` and ADR 0024 (superseding note).
- **Confidence:** high on the contradiction; the owner's intent is unknown.
